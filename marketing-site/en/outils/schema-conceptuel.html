Why the conceptual site model is mandatory

In any in-depth SSP diagnosis, the conceptual site model is not optional: it is the methodological foundation required by the national approach to managing polluted sites and soils.

  • It determines the relevance of the calculations: without an identified source-pathway-receptor link, no risk assessment is acceptable.
  • It structures the IEM and the Management Plan by making explicit the exposure pathways retained and excluded.
  • It secures traceability of the assumptions and the consistency of the whole study.
  • It provides an intuitive communication tool, readable by non-specialist audiences.

Frequently asked questions

Conceptual site model and health risk analysis

What is a conceptual site model for a polluted site?

The Conceptual Site Model is the structured representation of pollution sources, transfer pathways (soil, air, water) and exposed receptors (populations, ecosystems). It is the methodological foundation underpinning any health risk analysis carried out as part of an IEM or a Management Plan.

What is the link between the conceptual site model and the health risk analysis?

The health risk assessment (EQRS) reconstructs the source → pathway → receptor chain identified by the conceptual site model to quantify population exposure. Without a validated conceptual site model, no health risk assessment is acceptable under the national methodology for managing polluted sites and soils (MEEM/MTES 2017).

Is the conceptual site model mandatory for an IEM or a Management Plan?

Yes. The conceptual site model is the methodological foundation required by the national approach to managing polluted sites and soils for any Environmental Media State Interpretation (IEM) or Management Plan (PG).

Does the software connect to GMEP's other EQRS tools?

Yes, the Conceptual Site Model module connects natively to the outputs of EQRS V9 (human pathways) and EQRS ECOTOX (ecotoxicological pathways), with PNG/PDF export for direct insertion into reports.
