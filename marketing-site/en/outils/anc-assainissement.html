Method

From the in-situ soil to the selected system

The software first determines whether infiltration into the in-situ soil is possible, by cross-referencing the measured Kdim (Porchet tests), the dominant geological facies and the useful thickness of the relevant layer below the planned trench bottom.

Permeability classification (NF DTU 64.1)

Kdim (m/s) → mm/h conversion, then classification: K > 500 mm/h very permeable soil (spreading bed) · 50–500 mm/h permeable (infiltration trenches, 45 ml up to 5 PE + 9 ml/additional PE) · 30–50 mm/h moderately permeable (trenches, 50 ml + 10 ml/additional PE) · 15–30 mm/h poor permeability (parcel-level study, non-drained sand filter as a cautious alternative) · K < 15 mm/h impermeable soil (drained sand filter mandatory with an authorized outlet).

Confirmed infiltration incapacity

When the Kdim is < 15 mm/h and/or the dominant facies (clay, marl, clayey silt, compact shale) is present over a significant continuous thickness, the tool displays an explicit verdict of impossible infiltration and automatically determines the substitute system (vertical drained sand filter) with surface area, filter bed thickness and drain length.

Vulnerability rule — distance to intakes

Absolute, non-derogable prohibition on installation within 35 m of a declared intake or well used for human consumption (Arrêté of 7 September 2009 as amended by the Arrêté of 7 March 2012; Art. R.214-5 of the Environmental Code).

Attenuation model (Domenico solution)

For each receptor identified in the flow plume, the residual concentration is estimated using the analytical Domenico & Schwartz solution (dispersivities, retardation, first-order degradation), compared against the quality references for water intended for human consumption.

The software does not eliminate the need for a contradictory site visit or review by the competent SPANC. It does not replace the engineer's expertise.
